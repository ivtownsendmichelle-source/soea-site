In an emergency

Facing an evacuation? We're here to help.

Call early. The sooner you reach our coordinator, the more we can do, so don't wait for a mandatory order.

Call our coordinator, 24/7 during a fire 541-217-8235 If we can't answer right away, leave a message and we'll call you straight back.

We serve seven counties: Curry, Josephine, Jackson, Klamath & Douglas in Oregon, and Del Norte & Siskiyou in Northern California.

Please don't self-deploy

Don't post on social media asking for random help, and don't send yourself into a fire zone. Calling the coordinator keeps everyone safe and organized: animals, owners, and volunteers.

What we do

We evacuate, transport, and shelter.

Our all-volunteer team moves pets and livestock to safety when wildfire, flood, and other disasters strike. We cover seven counties across Southern Oregon and Northern California.

Evacuate

When fire, flood, or disaster threatens, trained volunteers get animals out before it's too late.

Transport

With trailers and trucks, we move animals large and small: horses, goats, chickens, dogs, cats, and more.

Shelter

Host farms across the region take evacuated animals in and care for them until it's safe to go home.

It all runs through one coordinator. Every need is matched to the right volunteer with the right equipment, so help arrives organized, never chaotic, and never self-deployed.

Our story

It started with one panicked phone call.

In 2014, a friend with an animal sanctuary called Linda Bacon in a panic. A fire was coming. Linda told her not to worry, she'd reach whoever evacuates animals, and then she found out no one did. So she posted on Facebook and asked if anyone with a trailer would come help.

The first volunteer signed on that day. Within two days there were thirty. In under a week, a hundred.

"When we hit 100 volunteers in less than a week, I was dancing on the stars. It was the best thing that ever happened."

Today, SOEA is roughly 300 volunteers across two states, coordinated from one place: Linda on the west side of the Cascades, Treasurer Maria Meister on the east. Still all volunteers. Still neighbors helping neighbors.

From the field

Rescues we'll never forget.

PHOTO #3Goats or livestock loading at night

Twenty goats in the dark

A daughter called late one evening. Her mother had a menagerie of goats, barn cats, and dogs, a fire heading straight for them, and she wanted to wait. Our team quietly staged down the road. When the yes finally came, they were ready. Pitch dark, a cougar prowling nearby, more than twenty goats to round up, and not one animal left behind.

PHOTO #4An emu, or an unusual rescue animal

"Auntz" the emu

A 25-year-old pet emu near Merlin, a maze of fences, a Level 3 evacuation, helicopters overhead. He would not load. He spat, he kicked, and one volunteer swore he was a dinosaur. At the shelter he still wouldn't leave the trailer, until Mary Ann looked him in the eye and said, "Auntz, if you don't get out of this trailer, we're having emu barbecue tomorrow." Out he came.

Questions

Common questions.

Is your help free?

Yes. We're volunteers, and we never charge to evacuate or shelter your animals.

What animals do you help?

Horses, livestock, and household pets, plus the unusual ones. No animal is too large or too small.

What area do you cover?

Seven counties: Curry, Josephine, Jackson, Klamath, and Douglas in Oregon, and Del Norte and Siskiyou in Northern California.

What if I have to leave before you arrive?

Call us anyway. If it's safe, leave gates unlocked and halters on, and leave a note with your animals' details and a phone number where we can reach you.

Can I volunteer without a trailer?

Yes. We also need trucks, drivers, host farms, and office help. Get on the response list and tell us what you can offer.

Should I just show up to help during a fire?

Please don't. Call the coordinator so we can send you where you're needed and keep everyone safe.

How do I get my animals ready before a fire?

Start with our Be Prepared guides, or call us and we'll talk it through with you.

Where your money goes

What your gift pays for.

We're all volunteers, so what you give goes straight to the work.

  1. 01

    Volunteer insurance

    Our volunteers drive toward the fire when everyone else is driving away. We insure them so they're protected. This year we came up short and couldn't cover it.

  2. 02

    Fuel and trucks

    Hauling a loaded trailer costs a fortune. We reimburse the volunteers who make load after load, often through gift cards from Grange Co-op and Tractor Supply.

  3. 03

    Feed for the animals we shelter

    When a family loses their barn, they lose their hay and tack too. We feed their animals until they can go home.

  4. 04

    Emergency supply runs

    When it's urgent, we move. One year we got a semi load of hay to Hornbrook two days before the snow closed the roads.
